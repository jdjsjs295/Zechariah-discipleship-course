ZECHARIAH

Walking in God’s Presence, Restoration, and Spirit-Powered Purpose

“Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.”
— Zechariah 4:6

Welcome to the course

This seven-week biblical discipleship course explores the book of Zechariah through the themes of God’s presence, protection, cleansing, restoration, obedience, dependence on the Holy Spirit, and faithful perseverance.

Course purpose: To help students recognize God’s presence, receive His correction and restoration, walk faithfully, and depend on His Spirit as they carry out what He has entrusted to them.

FormatSeven weekly lessons
IncludesScripture, teaching, questions, application, declarations
Course themeProtection → Restoration → Obedience → Spirit-empowered purpose

Study note: Read each passage in its biblical and historical context. The applications in this course are devotional reflections, not substitutes for careful interpretation of the text.

Week 1 · God’s presence

God Is in the Midst

Key Scripture: Zechariah 2:5

“For I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her.”

Central truth: God’s presence is greater than the protection we can create for ourselves. In Zechariah’s vision, the Lord promises to be both a protective wall around Jerusalem and the glory within her.

Study questions

  1. What does the image of a wall communicate in this passage?
  2. Why does God describe Himself as a wall of fire?
  3. What is the significance of God being “in the midst” of His people?
  4. How is trusting God’s protection different from trying to control every outcome?
  5. Where are you being challenged to trust God’s presence?

Practical application

Begin each day with prayer, asking God to make His presence the center of your decisions rather than allowing fear to direct them.

Declaration

God is my refuge and protector. His presence is with His people, and I can walk forward in faith rather than be governed by fear.

Week 2 · Responding to God’s call

Leaving What God Has Called You Out Of

Key Scriptures: Zechariah 2:6–7

Central truth: There are seasons when obedience requires movement. Zechariah calls the people still living in Babylon to come out and return. The historical setting matters: God was calling His people from exile toward the restoration of Jerusalem.

Important distinction: Leaving is not always rejection. In this passage, leaving Babylon is a response to God’s specific call. We should not assume every difficult situation means we must leave; decisions require Scripture, prayer, wisdom, and godly counsel.

Study questions

  1. What was God calling His people to leave?
  2. Why might responding to God’s call require courage?
  3. What can make people hesitant to move forward in obedience?
  4. How can we distinguish a biblical call to change from a reaction driven by fear or frustration?

Practical application

Pray: “Lord, show me where I need to obey You, what I need to release, and what faithful next step You are asking me to take.” Test what you sense against Scripture and wise counsel.

Declaration

I desire to obey God’s voice. I will not cling to what He has clearly called me to release, and I will seek His wisdom for each step.

Week 3 · Cleansing and grace

God Restores What Has Been Defiled

Key Scripture: Zechariah 3:1–5

Central truth: In Zechariah’s fourth vision, Joshua the high priest stands before the Angel of the LORD while the accuser stands to accuse him. Joshua’s filthy garments are removed and he is given clean garments. The vision portrays God’s gracious cleansing and restoration of the priesthood.

The movement in the vision: accusation → the LORD’s rebuke → removal of filthy garments → clean garments → renewed service.

Study questions

  1. Who is accusing Joshua?
  2. Who rebukes the accuser?
  3. What happens to Joshua’s filthy garments?
  4. What do the clean garments communicate in the vision?
  5. How does God’s gracious correction differ from condemnation?

Practical application

Bring sin, shame, and burdens honestly before God. Receive His forgiveness, respond to His correction with repentance, and remember that grace calls us into renewed faithfulness.

Declaration

I turn to the Lord for mercy and cleansing. I will receive His correction, reject hopeless condemnation, and walk in the grace He provides.

Week 4 · Faithful obedience

Restoration Requires Walking in God’s Ways

Key Scripture: Zechariah 3:7

“If thou wilt walk in my ways, and if thou wilt keep my charge…”

Central truth: Joshua’s cleansing is followed by a charge to walk in God’s ways and keep His requirements. Grace and responsibility belong together: God restores His servant and calls him to faithful service.

Study questions

  1. What does it mean to walk in God’s ways?
  2. What does it mean to keep His charge?
  3. Why should receiving grace lead us toward obedience?
  4. What does humble, accountable service look like today?

Practical exercise

Make two lists: “What God is teaching me” and “How I can respond faithfully.” Choose one concrete act of obedience to practice this week.

Declaration

I will respond to God’s grace with humility and obedience. I desire to walk in His ways and faithfully steward what He entrusts to me.

Week 5 · Dependence on the Spirit

It Will Not Be by Human Strength

Key Scripture: Zechariah 4:6

“Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Central truth: Zerubbabel faced the work of rebuilding the temple. God assured him that the work would be accomplished, not by human force or resources alone, but by the Spirit of the LORD. This is a call to dependence on God, not an instruction to neglect wise effort or practical responsibility.

Study questions

  1. What rebuilding work was Zerubbabel leading?
  2. What might “might” and “power” represent in this context?
  3. Why is dependence on the Spirit essential in God’s work?
  4. How can prayer and practical diligence work together?

Practical exercise

Before your daily responsibilities, pray: “Holy Spirit, lead me. Give me wisdom, courage, discernment, and strength to serve faithfully.” Identify one area where you need to stop relying on control and seek God’s help.

Declaration

I will not depend on my strength alone. I will seek the Holy Spirit’s guidance and faithfully do the work God has placed before me.

Week 6 · Perseverance

Do Not Despise the Day of Small Beginnings

Key Scripture: Zechariah 4:10

“For who hath despised the day of small things?”

Central truth: The rebuilding of the temple may have seemed unimpressive to some, but God’s work was not to be measured by appearances. Faithfulness matters even when progress seems small.

Study questions

  1. Why might people despise small beginnings?
  2. How can comparison discourage faithfulness?
  3. What small responsibility has God placed before you?
  4. How can you measure progress by faithfulness rather than speed?

Practical application

Identify one responsibility or act of service you have delayed because it seems too small. Take the next wise, faithful step.

Declaration

I will not despise small beginnings. I will be faithful with what is in my hands and trust God with the growth and outcome.

Week 7 · Completion and purpose

From Protection to Purpose

Key Scriptures: Zechariah 2:5; 3:1–7; 4:6–10

Central truth: These visions bring together God’s presence, cleansing, obedience, Spirit-enabled work, and perseverance. In the historical setting, God encouraged His people as they rebuilt the temple and renewed their life in the land.

Zechariah 2:5God’s presence and protection
Zechariah 3:1–5Cleansing and restoration
Zechariah 3:7Walking in God’s ways
Zechariah 4:6Dependence on the Spirit
Zechariah 4:9The work brought to completion
Zechariah 4:10Not despising small beginnings

Study questions

  1. How do God’s presence and cleansing prepare His people for service?
  2. Why must obedience accompany restoration?
  3. What does Spirit-enabled work look like in your daily life?
  4. How will you remain faithful when progress is slow?
Declaration

I will seek God’s presence, receive His grace, walk in obedience, and depend on His Spirit. I will remain faithful in the work set before me and trust Him with the outcome.

Course completion

Final Assignment — Your Zechariah Journey

Set aside time for prayer and reflection. Write your responses in a journal or discuss them with your group.

  1. Where have I seen God’s protection and presence?
  2. What might God be calling me to release or change?
  3. Where do I need to receive His cleansing, forgiveness, or correction?
  4. What does walking faithfully in God’s ways look like in my current season?
  5. Where have I been relying on my own strength?
  6. How can I depend more intentionally on the Holy Spirit?
  7. What small beginning should I stop overlooking?
  8. What is my next faithful step?
  9. What outcome do I need to entrust to God?

Course memory verse

Closing prayer

Father, thank You for Your Word and for the hope You gave Your people through Zechariah. Teach us to trust Your presence, receive Your cleansing, walk in obedience, and depend on Your Spirit. Give us courage to persevere in the work You have placed before us. Help us remain faithful in small beginnings and humble in every season. May our lives bring You glory. In Jesus’ name, amen.
